# OpenSSL transport qualification work

The [rewrite plan](../../docs/rust-rewrite-plan.md) requires transparent provider bytes, matching trust policy, cancellation and task continuity through successful downstream forwarding. The ordinary native client still uses Rustls. The OpenSSL constructors, raw pool, bounded buffering and encoder-handoff bridge remain test-only; passing their current subset does not authorize a production switch.

Use the current [encoder-handoff summary](../parity/measurements/openssl-buffered-handoff-summary.json) when choosing work. It retains 162 matching comparisons and 324 executions, including the four byte-loss cases that failed in the older [buffered-response report](../parity/measurements/openssl-buffered-response-summary.json). Do not repeat the old defect as a current finding or replace the historical report. Encoder admission, write attempts, source EOF, pool return and final downstream flush remain separate observations.

## Required lifecycle families

Each family needs the actual candidate constructor and gateway, explicit stimulus/observation barriers, bounded cleanup and retained source/executable identities. Primitive state-machine or buffer tests supplement these schedules. They cannot replace the gateway evidence below.

| Family | Required evidence and ownership boundary |
| --- | --- |
| HEAD, no-body statuses and final data | Exercise HEAD, 204, 304, zero-length responses, final-data `is_end_stream`, trailers and clean EOF through the candidate. Preserve method/status framing and provider bytes; only complete consumer ownership may return a raw connection. No-body submission still needs successful header flush. |
| Blocked final flush and continuity | Deliver a complete valid provider tool response, then hold the downstream flush after all framing bytes are accepted. Confirm neither successful delivery nor a replacement model while flush is pending. Release successfully, fail the flush, cancel and expire the request in separate schedules. Only the successful control may replace the previously confirmed tool owner. |
| Attachment and failure races | Cover failure before headers, acquired headers before attachment, a response queued for encoding, attachment failure and cancellation at each boundary. A latched body failure must preserve acquired status while preventing a false success; dropping a stale response must not affect another request. |
| Pipelined A/B ownership | Execute actual concurrent service futures on one keep-alive connection, including B becoming ready before A. Bind submission, failure, EOF and flush to their exact response sequence. Retired A notifications and deadlines must not close or confirm B. |
| Buffered sibling and raw/fetch separation | Exercise two simultaneous same-origin responses, a different origin and fetch alongside raw forwarding. Cancellation, parser errors, session invalidation and pool retirement must affect only the owning request/connection. Raw-pool-only evidence does not qualify the buffered constructor. |
| Eight saturated responses and repeated cancellation | Hold eight real downstream consumers, reach declared buffer pressure, then cancel in independent orders and repeat. Account for retained aliases, producer tasks, sockets, timers and pool/session owners through cleanup. A per-buffer payload limit alone is insufficient. |
| Long legal streaming and aggregate allocation | Forward a generated legal stream with exact incremental byte count/digest and complete framing. Account for application blocks, Hyper frame backing, TLS state, encoder buffers, retained headers/aliases and metadata across all live owners. Keep total memory qualification pending until every term and recovery is justified. |

The [final-flush regressions](../crates/autorouter-runtime/src/server_failed_flush_contracts.rs) exercise 96 synthetic schedules through the actual buffered constructor: HTTP, negotiated TLS 1.2 and TLS 1.3; ordinary completion and attached request-terminal ownership; successful flushing, flush error, cancellation and absolute deadline. The original 24 schedules hold an instrumented downstream writer after the complete JSON tool response and HTTP framing have been accepted. Clean buffered EOF leaves the completion registry and replacement attempt pending. Only successful flushing replaces the previously confirmed Opus tool owner with Sonnet; the other outcomes preserve it. The tests verify callback, writer, task, socket, buffer and cache cleanup.

The other 72 schedules cover empty JSON 200, 204 and 304 responses. They hold the provider socket open independently of clean body EOF, then hold the downstream writer at complete empty framing or the header-only final flush. Neither that EOF nor accepted framing can finish the pending attempt. Successful empty delivery releases the attempt once without execution confirmation or replacing the prior Opus owner; failed flushing, cancellation and deadline expiry also preserve that owner. Status and end-to-end headers remain intact, with zero application bytes and no forwarded hop-by-hop headers or trailers.

The [direct constructor controls](../crates/autorouter-runtime/src/tls_roots/openssl_spike/raw_pool_buffered_tests.rs) separately cover HEAD with a representation length, zero-length GET, 204 and 304 over all three protocols. They check negotiated TLS, exact method/status/length and clean pool return without consumer body polling while the provider socket remains held. These twelve controls do not add forwarded HEAD routes to the gateway.

The [consumer/pool controls](../parity/measurements/native-consumer-pool-handoff-summary.json) add six actual-constructor schedules over those three protocols. Clean prefetch leaves the raw connection reserved until the consumer takes the final data frame. Its end hint returns the reservation without an extra EOF poll; request B then arrives on the same accepted provider socket. A retained clone of A's data remains charged through B and transport cleanup until the clone drops. The paired abandonment control drops A without polling, observes retirement and requires a new accepted peer for B. These tests supplement the existing primitive body controls. Gateway final-data ownership, delayed trailers, close-delimited EOF and broader concurrent reuse still need their own schedules.

These controls use bounded synthetic responses, isolated fixture certificates for TLS and a scripted downstream writer. They do not establish all response shapes, real downstream socket failures, certificate-policy parity, pipeline ownership or the complete family matrix. The ordinary failed-framing control remains alongside them, and the [deadline regression](../crates/autorouter-runtime/src/server_deadline_contracts.rs) independently covers a held writer. Keep [`CompletionRegistry`](../crates/autorouter-runtime/src/transport_completion.rs) as the final delivery authority. Repeat the focused module from `rust/` with:

```sh
cargo test --locked --package autorouter-runtime server::failed_flush_contracts
```

## Remaining policy and session work

Retain the exact old session/idle comparison outcomes: original sessions 13/18, intent sessions 15/18, idle-close 4/10 and raw-pool original sessions 15/18. These refer to distinct retained constructors and executables. Fresh comparisons must explicitly select the candidate being evaluated and preserve the frozen Node inputs, event/call assertions and complete cleanup. A controlled race can explain an ownership boundary, but cannot turn an unresolved unbarriered comparison into a passing one.

The historical [buffered replay checkpoint](../parity/measurements/native-buffered-session-idle-replay-summary.json) runs both complete drivers against one newly compiled optimized runtime library-test executable and the unchanged frozen Node reference. Both session runs match 13/18; both idle-close runs match 4/10. All application contracts and cleanup checks pass, but five session comparisons and all six unbarriered idle comparisons per constructor remain failures. The same executable also passes all 162 byte/handoff comparisons across 324 executions. This extends observation to the actual buffered Gateway; it does not resolve session/idle parity or replace historical reports.

Both `check-openssl-sessions.mjs` and `check-openssl-idle-close.mjs` accept a closed `--constructor=legacy|buffered` selector, defaulting to `legacy`. Pass the frozen-reference directory and the actual runtime library-test executable as their two positional arguments, using the pinned Node 22.14.0/OpenSSL 3.0.15+quic reference runtime. Require the complete ordered 18/10 case inventories, original observed/application/cleanup predicates and clean child exit status; the session report's `passed` field alone is insufficient. The new child entrypoints return immediately without their synthetic control environment during ordinary unit runs, so those unit passes are not protocol evidence. The idle observer rejects simultaneous legacy Intent ownership before assignment or I/O.

The preceding [qualified cancellation checkpoint](../parity/measurements/native-qualified-cancellation-bridge-summary.json) supplies that missing link for the private buffered backend. Both cancellation cases now match Node, improving the complete buffered session comparison to 15/18. Each retains the first physical `Downstream(ReadEof)` cause, one `Applied(Requested)` outcome, one session error close and clean teardown. The unchanged legacy control stays at 13/18; both idle comparisons stay at 4/10. The two abrupt-after-complete cases, TLS 1.3 rejected-host observation and all six unbarriered idle cases per constructor still fail.

Twenty-five added tests cover admission, stale/retired capabilities, callback ordering, independent failure settlement, split reader/writer wakes, preheader errors, late attachment and body completion. Six direct constructor controls exercise HTTP, TLS 1.2 and TLS 1.3: an already published reservation is aborted before HTTP admission with zero HTTP bytes, or an absolute deadline claims it while headers are withheld. Dispatch counters supplement actual peer closure; they are not physical shutdown proof. A real acquired Entry/reservation control separately holds a first failure inside the final body poll and verifies ownership remains until the claim completes.

The first failure action runs outside locks before request or producer cancellation. Completion retirement arbitrates with failure under the same request mutex; deferred cleanup owns the exact lease and sender in the existing task registry, with a weak client reference and explicit lease-before-sender drop order. Ordinary body drop, generic cancellation, upstream failure and prepublication observations do not gain abort authority. Explicit destruction of the whole client/connection remains a separate shutdown path. An artificial read error supplying more than 64 KiB of simultaneous progress is preserved but marked observer-failed and unqualified; it cannot qualify this bridge.

The complete checkpoint passes 1,576 Rust tests, strict Clippy, formatting, 645 Node tests and package checks, preserving every old named test outcome. All 162 byte/handoff comparisons still pass through the same fresh optimized test executable. Current source archive/restore verifies 714 raw inputs and 113 restored reference files under the unchanged source cap. These results do not promote OpenSSL to production or close the broader lifecycle, platform, performance and release requirements.

The current [handshake egress checkpoint](../parity/measurements/native-handshake-egress-ci-barrier-summary.json) resolves the TLS 1.3 rejected-host observation. Node checks the peer before draining its final outgoing handshake flight; the earlier direct native BIO flushed it first. A private bounded wrapper now retains at most 64 KiB, drains preceding flights before nonempty reads, and releases final egress after the unchanged chain, exact hostname, DH and offered-session checks. Successful flushing precedes ticket acceptance and connection publication. Rejection and cancellation drop the same socket and pending bytes. Its primitive shutdown control concerns the wrapper transport, not arbitrary `SSL_shutdown` before acceptance.

Buffered sessions now match 16/18, legacy sessions 14/18 and both idle inventories 4/10. All prior application observations and cancellation ownership remain, apart from the corrected hostname handshake vector. The first final-binary byte/handoff trial retains two reference-owner deadline failures (160/162); one declared unchanged full rerun passes 162/162 across 324 executions. Seven new controls and the full 1,583-test workspace pass. Four failed parent CI jobs exposed a single-poll fixture assumption; a spawned-task budget-exhaustion control and bounded actual-driver rejection barrier repair that test without weakening its old assertions. Fresh CI remains required.

The older fresh-handshake experiment reported 223 matching cases, excluding three OpenSSL configuration modes. Two new source-v2 policy trials remain failed at 222/223 and 220/223 because of reference startup/request observations; their Rust outcome vectors agree, but they are not a combined pass or final-artifact qualification. Referenced historical raw 223-case reports are unavailable locally, so no historical row-level equivalence is claimed. `OPENSSL_CONF`, `--openssl-config` and `--openssl-shared-config` still need fresh-process configuration/loading/error/privacy evidence before support is claimed. Do not weaken certificate or handshake verification, retry requests, infer socket ownership from a weak open flag, or change the oracle to obtain a match.

Promotion additionally requires the production feature/dependency and vendor-provenance review, full protocol and installed-archive regressions, supported-platform TLS execution, resource/performance gates and explicitly invoked provider canaries from the full plan. Historical experimental binaries do not qualify a rebuilt shipping binary. Keep source/build identity, exact package bytes and rollback evidence bound to the final candidate.
